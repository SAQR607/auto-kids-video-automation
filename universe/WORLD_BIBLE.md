# WORLD BIBLE — Fernwood Valley

> The canonical world for **Fernwood Friends**. Every episode happens here.
> Machine-readable contract: `universe/manifest.json`.

## One-line premise
A hidden woodland valley where small animal friends live, explore, and slowly
discover why their home glows with kindness.

## Geography
Fernwood sits in a valley bowl ringed by tall pines, with **the River** cutting
through from the Mossy Ravine down past the village to the eastern pools.
Everything a story needs is within a short walk:

```
Mossy Ravine & Old Root Bridge  (north-west, adventurous)
        |
Whispering Stream  ----->  Hollow Oak Village (center, hub)
        |                        |
Glimmer Meadow (east)      Willowby's Burrow (under the village green)
        |
Glimmer Cave (far east hill, season mystery)
```

- **The Village** sits inside and around one enormous ancient oak ("Hollow Oak").
  Homes are round doors in roots and trunks; mushroom lamps line the paths.
- **The River** feeds Whispering Stream; stepping stones cross it. Marlow's
  favourite spot is the wide pool below the third stone.
- **Glimmer Meadow** is the "wonder meadow": tall grass, flowers, and — at
  dusk — the Glimmer (soft floating lights).
- **The Glimmer Cave** is sealed by a root-lattice door that only opens when
  four friends stand together (season-finale payoff).

## The Glimmer (central wonder engine)
- Soft lights that drift up from the meadow when someone in Fernwood shows
  real kindness, courage, or honesty — even small acts.
- The valley "remembers" kindness: older Glimmer sites (cave stones, meadow,
  stream ripples) glow brighter as acts accumulate.
- Nobody understands it at first. Season 1 is the question: *Why does Fernwood
  glimmer?* The answer is never spoken by a narrator — the characters discover
  it through observation, testing, and helping each other.
- Rules (never break): the Glimmer never frightens, never punishes, never
  grants wishes. It only *responds*. It cannot be bought or hoarded.

## Time & rhythm
- Stories span one day mostly; evening/night scenes allowed (crickets, lantern
  light) but always end in safety.
- Weather: gentle rain, morning mist, breeze. Storms only as mild background
  tension (a drizzle that soaks a map), never scary thunder drama.

## Society & daily life
- Fernwood is a gift economy: everyone helps everyone (badger builds, hare
  knows stories, otter fishes, wren scouts, squirrel runs errands).
- Governance: informal — Willowby is consulted, never obeyed.
- No money, no shops; trading is barter of baked goods, tools, songs, news.
- Technology: no electricity; lanterns, whistles, rope, pulleys, paper notes.

## Season arc — "The Glimmer Question" (13 long episodes)
| EP | Crumb |
|----|-------|
| 01 | First Glimmer seen over the meadow after Juni helps Pipkin |
| 02 | Lights don't appear for showing off — only for helping |
| 03 | Wren maps glow spots; a pattern points east |
| 04 | A glowing **compass stone** is found under the third stepping stone |
| 05 | The stone points toward the sealed cave root-door |
| 06 | Measuring the glow: brightness scales with number of friends helping |
| 07 | Faint humming from the cave at dusk |
| 08 | Willowby's oldest book mentions "the valley's ledger of kindness" |
| 09 | Pebble stacks appear overnight on the meadow (someone else is out there?) |
| 10 | The stack-builder is revealed: old Fern gathering stones to thank the Glimmer |
| 11 | Storm drizzle washes out the path — four friends rebuild it, glow surge |
| 12 | The root-door stirs when all four touch it together |
| 13 | The cave heart: a stone that glows with *everyone's* kindness — and a new question for Season 2 |

Each long episode ALSO tells a complete stand-alone story (learning spine +
resolution). Shorts are self-contained beats — no arc knowledge required.

## Hard world rules
1. No villains, no weapons, no chasing in fear — conflict is misunderstanding,
   lost objects, mild weather, self-doubt.
2. Solutions come from kindness, observation, teamwork, or a clever tool.
3. Every question raised for kids gets an on-screen answer before the episode
   ends (curiosity is rewarded, never left hanging).
4. Adults/elders never rescind children's feelings; they help investigate.
5. Nature is accurate at a glance (animal behaviours plausible, seasons
   coherent) even though characters talk.
